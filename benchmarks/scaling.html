P2CLPFD scaling

4 suppliers, quad sourcing, 5% minimum share per supplier. Everything else held fixed.

Where the time goes

quantity, not catalogue size

Sourcing ten times as many items costs ten times the time — that scales fine. Ordering ten times the quantity of one item costs far more than ten times, and that is the ceiling you hit first. Restricting awards to a 5% grid removes quantity from the problem altogether: 0.32s at 20,000 units, against 37s at 400 units without it.

Time against number of items

Per-item cost is flat at roughly 105 ms — 115 ms at 100 items, 105 ms at 3,000. Measured growth is x0.99, i.e. linear: doubling the catalogue doubles the time and no worse, because nothing here ties one item to another.

Time against demand per item

With quantities free, 40× the demand costs 1333× the time — measured growth x1.90, near-quadratic, because the quantity sets each variable's domain and therefore the space the search must cover. Restrict awards to a 5% grid and the curve flattens: x0.16, still 0.32s at 20,000 units — 50× further out than the free run could reach at all.

What one portfolio-wide rule costs

Adding a cap on any supplier's share of total volume ties every item into a single search — minimizing a sum couples everything the sum touches, so the per-item decomposition no longer applies.

> 550s

at just 100 items, against 11s for the same items with no portfolio rule. The sweep could not produce a curve because its very first point exceeded the budget. This is the case decompose.pl still cannot split, and it is the next thing worth fixing.

The realistic setup: a portfolio cap plus an award grid

No supplier above 30% of total volume, awards on a 5% grid, 1,000 units per item. A portfolio cap ties every item together on paper — but once the catalogue is large enough the cheapest supplier varies from item to item, the cap stops binding, and the solver can prove each item optimal on its own again. That makes it linear at about 209 ms per item, so roughly 1,542 items fits inside a 5-minute wait. Below about eight items it is a different story: with only a handful of items one supplier wins most of them, the cap really does bite, and the solve stalls — 2, 3, 5, 6 items exceeded the budget.

Method

Each point is one solve() on generated data, timed in a fresh process so a run that overruns can be killed — CLP(FD) labeling does not reliably yield to Prolog's own timeout. Prices rotate per item so the optimum is a real choice rather than a tie. Capacity is set to demand, so it never binds. Timeout 520s per solve.

Reproduce with python scripts/benchmark.py, then python scripts/make_chart.py.
